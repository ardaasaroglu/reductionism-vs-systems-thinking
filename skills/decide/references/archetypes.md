# Classic system archetypes

Name an archetype only if the loop map matches it. If none fits, write "none". Sources: Senge, The Fifth Discipline; Meadows, Thinking in Systems. Each link below is written "cause -> effect (sign)".

## Fixes that fail

* **Product story:** a discount lifts this month's conversions, and customers learn to wait for the next one.
* **Loop structure:** a fast B loop (problem -> fix (+), fix -> problem (-)) plus a delayed R loop through a side effect (problem -> fix (+), fix -> side effect (+), side effect -> problem (+)).
* **Signal:** the problem returns after each fix, a little worse: full-price conversion falling between offers.

## Shifting the burden

* **Product story:** a support chatbot absorbs tickets, so the product bugs behind them never reach the roadmap.
* **Loop structure:** two B loops on one problem, a fast symptomatic fix and a slow fundamental fix, plus an R loop where the symptomatic fix weakens the fundamental one (symptomatic fix -> fundamental fix (-)).
* **Signal:** the symptomatic fix grows while the root cause does not move: bot deflection rising while tickets per 1,000 accounts stay flat.

## Limits to growth

* **Product story:** a referral program grows fast, then slows as referrers run out of friends who are not users yet.
* **Loop structure:** an R growth loop (users -> invites (+), invites -> users (+)) meets a B loop through a limited resource (invites -> untapped friends (-), untapped friends -> acceptance (+), acceptance -> users (+), users -> invites (+)).
* **Signal:** growth slows while effort per unit rises: invite acceptance falling cohort by cohort.

## Success to the successful

* **Product story:** top-ranked sellers in a marketplace get more visibility, so they sell more and rank higher, and new sellers never get a start.
* **Loop structure:** two R loops that share one allocation (success of A -> resources to A (+), resources to A -> success of A (+)), where more resources to A means fewer to B (resources to A -> resources to B (-)).
* **Signal:** concentration rising: the share of sales going to the top 10% of sellers, and new-seller survival falling.

## Escalation

* **Product story:** two apps match each other's sign-up bonuses until both lose money on every new user.
* **Loop structure:** two B loops that join into one R loop (our offer -> their threat (+), their threat -> their offer (+), their offer -> our threat (+), our threat -> our offer (+)).
* **Signal:** offer size rising on both sides while market share stays flat.

## Eroding goals

* **Product story:** the team misses its page speed target, so it relaxes the target instead of fixing the pages.
* **Loop structure:** two B loops on one gap: fix it (gap -> corrective action (+), corrective action -> performance (+), performance -> gap (-)) or lower the goal (gap -> pressure to lower the goal (+), pressure -> goal (-), goal -> gap (+)).
* **Signal:** the target moves after each miss, and the metric drifts down over quarters.

## Tragedy of the commons

* **Product story:** every team sends push notifications from the same app, each one lifts its own clicks, and users turn notifications off for all of them.
* **Loop structure:** an R loop per team (team sends -> team gain (+), team gain -> team sends (+)) on one shared resource, with a B loop through it (team sends -> user tolerance (-), user tolerance -> team gain (+)).
* **Signal:** gain per send falling while total sends rise: opt-out rate and clicks per notification, by month.

## Growth and underinvestment

* **Product story:** usage grows faster than support or infrastructure, quality drops, growth slows, and the slowdown is read as a reason not to invest.
* **Loop structure:** an R growth loop limited by a B loop through capacity (demand -> quality (-), quality -> demand (+)), plus a delayed B loop where weaker demand lowers investment in capacity.
* **Signal:** quality falling before growth slows: response time or latency rising, then capacity budgets cut after a slow quarter.
